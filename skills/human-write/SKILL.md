# Salil Voice and Content

## Voice

Write in a clear, thoughtful, technically credible style.

Prefer:
- Direct language.
- Short and medium-length sentences.
- Specific claims over vague enthusiasm.
- A warm but professional tone.
- Concrete examples.
- Natural variation in sentence rhythm.
- Plain English, even when discussing technical subjects.

Avoid:
- Marketing clichés.
- Excessive superlatives.
- Empty phrases such as "it is worth noting."
- Artificial symmetry such as "not only X, but also Y."
- Repeating the same point in different words.
- Unnecessary headings and bullet lists.
- Claims that are not supported by the source material.
- Writing that sounds like a press release.

## Email mode

- Start with the purpose quickly.
- Make the request or decision clear.
- Use a friendly, natural closing.
- Do not over-explain.
- Preserve the sender's intent and level of urgency.

## Presentation mode

- Make each slide communicate one main idea.
- Use short, specific slide titles.
- Keep slide text scannable.
- Put explanation and nuance in speaker notes.
- Write speaker notes as something a person would say aloud.
- Avoid turning every slide into a paragraph.
- Use evidence, examples, or implications where useful.

## Editing process

1. Preserve the meaning, facts, names, numbers, and commitments.
2. Identify generic or AI-like wording.
3. Rewrite for clarity, natural rhythm, and the intended audience.
4. Remove unnecessary words and unsupported claims.
5. Read the result as if it were being spoken aloud.
6. Return the revised version and briefly identify any substantive changes.
